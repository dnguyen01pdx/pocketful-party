Here's a funny thing about starting med school: you finally make it in, and then almost right away someone mentions ERAS, program signals and the Match, and you realize there's a whole second application waiting at the end. If that made your stomach drop a little, same here!

I'm a first-year, so residency applications are still ahead of me too. I wanted to understand the whole road before I'm in the middle of it, so I mapped it out the way I did for med school applications: official dates first, then the advice upperclassmen and residents keep repeating. ## The two systems you'll deal with

Residency applications run through two separate systems, which confused me at first:

- **ERAS** (the AAMC's Electronic Residency Application Service) is where you build your application, called MyERAS, and send it to programs.
- **The NRMP** (National Resident Matching Program) runs the Match itself. You register there separately, rank programs after interviews, and the Match algorithm pairs applicants with programs.

So you apply through one and match through the other. Once that clicked, the timeline made a lot more sense!

## The real dates for the 2027 cycle

These come from the AAMC's 2027 ERAS Residency Timeline and the NRMP's 2027 Main Residency Match applicant calendar. That's the cycle happening right now, for residency starting in 2027. Dates shift a little every year, so treat these as anchors and check the official calendars for your own cycle.

- **June 4, 2026:** the 2027 ERAS season opened, and applicants could start working on MyERAS.
- **September 2, 2026:** applicants could start submitting applications to programs.
- **September 15, 2026:** NRMP registration opened.
- **September 23, 2026:** programs could start reviewing applications and MSPEs (your school's evaluation letter, sometimes called the dean's letter).
- **January 29, 2027:** the NRMP's standard registration deadline. Registering after that adds a $50 late fee.
- **February 1, 2027:** ranking opens.
- **March 3, 2027, 9 p.m. ET:** rank order list certification deadline.
- **March 15, 2027:** Match Week starts, and applicants find out whether they matched (but not where yet).
- **March 19, 2027, 12 p.m. ET:** Match Day!

For fees, the AAMC's 2027 ERAS fee page lists $11 per program for your first 30 programs in a specialty and $30 per program after that, with pricing that resets for each additional specialty. Always confirm the current numbers before you pay for anything.

## Early clinical years: collect the raw material

The most common advice I've seen from people who've been through it is to stop treating residency as a fourth-year problem. Not in a stressful way! It's more that the best application material comes from things you'd forget otherwise.

- **Keep a running list** of patients, cases and projects that stuck with you. Even one line each. These become your experiences, your personal statement and your interview stories.
- **Write down who saw your work up close.** An attending who watched you present every day for four weeks can write a far more specific letter than someone you met once.
- **Explore specialties you're curious about** while you still can, and ask your school how and when away rotations get planned.

## Winter and spring before you apply: decisions

This is where the big choices happen, and honestly it sounds like the hardest stretch emotionally.

- **Pick your specialty**, or a main specialty plus a backup if your advisors suggest it. Talk to an advisor and to residents in that field, since what each specialty expects (letters, exams, research) varies a lot.
- **Ask for letters early.** Same rules as med school: ask in person or by a thoughtful email, send your CV and a few points you'd love them to mention, and give a deadline a few weeks before you really need it.
- **Start your personal statement.** The advice I see repeated most is to keep it specific and honest. Program directors read a lot of "I knew I wanted to be a ___ when..." openings, so a real moment and what it taught you goes a long way.

## June to September: MyERAS and submission

When ERAS opens, start filling out MyERAS early. It's long, and writing up your experiences takes more time than people expect.

Two things to look up for your specialty during this stretch:

- **Program signals.** Many specialties let you send a limited number of signals to show programs you're especially interested in them. The number and rules vary by specialty, so check MyERAS and your specialty's guidance.
- **Geographic preferences.** MyERAS also lets you share where in the country you'd like to train.

Then submit as early as you can in September. Programs begin reviewing on a set date (September 23 in the 2027 cycle), so having everything in before then means you're there from the very first day of review. And register for the Match!

## October to January: interview season

This is the busy one. A few habits that come up again and again:

- **Log every invite** and answer quickly, since popular dates fill up.
- **Test your camera, light and sound** before virtual interviews.
- **Write notes right after each interview.** By January, programs start blending together, and your notes are what you'll rank from.

If you want a refresher on interview basics, my [med school interview prep checklist](../med-school-interview-prep/) covers the classic questions and the virtual setup, and most of it carries over.

## February and March: rank, certify, Match

When ranking opens, rank programs in the order you truly prefer. The advice from people who've matched is to not try to game the algorithm, because it's built so that honest ranking works in your favor. Then certify your list well before the deadline. An uncertified list doesn't count, and nobody wants that stress at 8:55 p.m.!

Then comes Match Week, and finally Match Day. I can't wait to see that day for myself someday, and I'm cheering for everyone who gets there this March.

## The short version

Collect stories and letter writers during your clinical years, pick your specialty and ask for letters in winter and spring, start MyERAS when it opens in June, submit early in September, keep good notes through interview season, then rank honestly and certify early. It's a long road, but you don't have to walk it all at once. One step at a time, and you've totally got this!
