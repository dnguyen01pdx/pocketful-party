If you've scrolled through study-with-me videos and aesthetic desk photos lately, you know the look: soft lamp, matching notebooks, a plant, two monitors, an iced coffee sweating in exactly the right spot. I love those too! But here's the thing nobody says out loud: the photo-ready desk and the desk that helps you get through a hard week of lectures aren't always the same desk.

I'm a first-year, so I'm still figuring out my own setup, and honestly it changes every block. This is what I've pulled together from the tips that keep getting agreed with in med student forums and from upperclassmen, written the way I'd say it to a friend who just moved into a new apartment for school. There's a free one-page checklist at the end, and the best part is that most of it costs nothing.

## Start with where, not what

Before you buy a single thing, figure out where you'll actually study. The advice I see repeated most is to have more than one spot, each with a job:

- **A home base for deep work.** A desk or a table where you watch lectures and do question blocks. Same place, same time, most days, so your brain learns that sitting there means work.
- **A backup spot.** The library, a study room at school, a quiet corner of a coffee shop. When home gets distracting (or lonely), a change of scenery can rescue a whole afternoon.
- **A light-review spot.** Somewhere comfy for Anki reviews on tired evenings. That's where the couch gets to be part of the plan!
- **Bed is for sleep.** Lots of people say that watching lectures in bed makes both studying and sleeping worse, and once you've tried it a few times, it's hard to argue.

## Set up the desk (the basics that matter)

You really don't need a fancy setup. A few small things make long days a lot more comfortable:

- **Get your screen closer to eye level.** Looking down at a laptop for hours is rough on your neck. A cheap laptop stand works, and so does a stack of old textbooks (finally, a use for them!).
- **Use a second screen if you already have one.** Lots of students love lecture on one screen and notes or Anki on the other. If you don't have one, splitting your laptop screen works fine too. It's a nice-to-have, not a must.
- **Pick a chair you can sit in for a two-hour block.** If you're shifting around every ten minutes, a pillow behind your back or a different chair may help more than any new gadget. And if something keeps hurting, please check in with your school's student health services.
- **Fix the light.** A desk lamp plus daylight when you can get it. Squinting at a screen in a dark room at 11 p.m. is a fast way to feel exhausted.
- **Clear the surface.** Only today's stuff out. A cluttered desk is one more thing your brain has to ignore.

## Cut the distractions (this is the big one)

If you only change one thing, make it this one. The tip I see agreed with most, by far, is **put your phone in another room** during study blocks. Not face down next to you. Another room! It feels dramatic the first day and normal by the third.

A few more that help:

- **Turn on a site blocker** during study blocks so you can't drift into scrolling between lectures.
- **Use headphones or earplugs** if you live with other people or study somewhere loud.
- **Tell your roommates your study hours.** People are usually happy to help when they know.

## Keep the little things within reach

Every trip to the kitchen is a chance to get lost on the way back (we've all done it). Before a study block, grab:

- A water bottle and a snack
- Your charger and a long cord
- A whiteboard or scratch paper for drawing out pathways
- Today's to-do list, written down

If you want help deciding what goes on that list each day, my [weekly med school study schedule](../med-school-weekly-study-schedule/) walks through how to lay out a week of lectures, Anki and review.

## Now for the fun part: make it yours

Okay, the aesthetic part does matter a little! A space you actually like sitting in is easier to come back to. Keep it simple, though: one thing you love looking at, like a plant, a photo of the people cheering you on, or a print you like. And a lot of students tape up a small note with why they chose medicine. On a rough day, that note does more than any matching notebook set.

## Reset it every day

The easiest habit to keep is a **five-minute reset at your stop time.** Clear the desk, put things back, and set out tomorrow's first task so you can start without deciding anything. Future-morning you will be so grateful!

Then, after two weeks, check in with yourself: where did you focus best? Home, the library, a study room? Lean into whatever actually worked, even if it's not the spot you expected. And if a hard block has you feeling stuck no matter where you sit, my [weekly reset worksheet](../med-school-study-motivation/) might help, and my [Anki beginner guide](../anki-for-med-school-beginners/) has a simple daily routine to go with your new setup.

## How to use the printable

The checklist is one page, split into eight parts:

- **Pick your spots:** home base, backup spot, light-review spot.
- **Set up the desk:** screen height, second screen, chair, light and a clear surface.
- **Cut the distractions:** phone, site blocker, noise and roommates.
- **Keep within reach:** water, charger, scratch paper and today's list.
- **Before each study block:** a quick four-step start, including one task per block and a timer for your break.
- **Make it yours:** one thing you love, a plant or photo, and your "why" note.
- **Reset and check in:** the daily reset and a two-week check-in.
- **My one or two fixes this week:** two blank lines for what you'll change first.

Check off what you already have, pick one or two things to fix this week, and leave the rest. You don't need everything at once!

## The short version

Pick a home base, a backup spot and a light-review spot. Get your screen up, your light right and your desk clear. Put your phone in another room. Keep water, a charger and scratch paper close. Add one thing you love, reset the desk every night, and check after two weeks where you focus best. The best study space is the one that helps you show up, and you're already doing that!
