Friendsgiving has a different energy from the family version. Nobody's grandma is watching, the guest list is your actual friends, and dinner tends to run long. What it usually lacks is a game that's easy enough to start mid-conversation and loose enough that nobody has to learn rules.

Would you rather is that game. These 24 cards are all about the stuff everyone has opinions on: stuffing versus pie, who does the dishes, whether 2 pm is a real dinner time. They're clean enough for a mixed crowd and silly enough to start real arguments.

## What you get

Three pages, eight cards per page, with dashed cut lines. Each card has two options and a little "or" in the middle. Print on cardstock if you have it so the cards survive a spilled drink. Regular paper works too.

A few of the questions in the stack:

- Wash every dish tonight, or dry every dish tonight?
- Have unlimited leftovers, or unlimited dessert?
- Host 20 people in a tiny apartment, or 4 people in a mansion?
- Burn the rolls, or forget to thaw the turkey?

## Three ways to play

### 1. The classic (no scoring)

Pass the stack around. Whoever holds it reads the top card, everyone answers, and the reader has to defend the less popular choice for 30 seconds. Then the stack moves left. This is the version for the table while people are still eating.

### 2. Majority rules (with points)

Everyone writes their answer secretly on a scrap of paper or in their phone notes. Flip at the same time. Anyone in the majority scores a point. Ties mean nobody scores. Play to 10. It's surprisingly competitive, because you start guessing what the room thinks instead of what you think.

### 3. Who said that?

Good for a group that knows each other well. One person answers a card privately and hands their answer to the reader. Everyone else guesses who answered and why. Correct guesses earn a point, and the answerer earns a point for every person they fool.

## Tips for a smooth game

- **Start after the main course.** People are full, sitting down, and ready to stay put.
- **Let the host go first.** It signals everyone's allowed to be a little ridiculous.
- **Add your own cards.** Write your own on the blank backs of the cards. Inside jokes from your friend group always land better than anything we can write.
- **Keep it moving.** If a card isn't sparking anything, skip it. Nobody needs a ten-minute debate on cranberry sauce. (Although it might happen anyway.)

## Hosting a big Friendsgiving?

With 12 or more people, split into two tables and give each table a page of cards. After 15 minutes, swap the pages. Then bring everyone together for a final "majority rules" round with the cards that caused the most arguments.

## When the conversation gets real

Some of these cards look silly and then turn into the best talk of the night. "Dinner with your favorite celebrity or your childhood best friend" has a way of turning into stories about who you used to be. Let those moments run. The point of the game isn't to get through the stack; it's to get the table talking. If one card sparks twenty minutes of stories, that card won.

## Make it a tradition

Keep the cards in an envelope with the date and the guest list written on it. Pull them out next year and see whose answers changed. The person who switched from "dry every dish" to "wash every dish" has clearly grown as a person.
