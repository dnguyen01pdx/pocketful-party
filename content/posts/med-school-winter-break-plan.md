Winter break is close, and if you're anything like me, part of your brain is already planning the naps while another part is quietly asking, "Shouldn't I be using this time to get ahead?" That little voice is so common in med school! You've spent months in a rhythm where every free hour felt like it should go to studying, and turning that off is weirdly hard.

I'm a first-year too, so this will be my first med school winter break as well. I went looking for what upperclassmen and med student forums actually recommend, and the answer was surprisingly consistent. So here's the plan I'm building for myself, written the way I'd share it with a friend in my class. There's a free one-page planner at the end.

## The short answer: rest is the main job

The advice I saw repeated most, by far: **for most first-years, winter break is for rest.** Not "rest, but also do 200 practice questions a day." Actual rest. Sleep, people you love, things that have nothing to do with medicine.

There are a couple of reasons that keeps coming up:

- **The next block is coming either way.** Starting it already tired makes the whole spring harder.
- **Breaks are when you recover.** Months of long days add up, and a real break is how you refill the tank. Burnout is the fastest way to lose a whole week later.
- **You've earned it!** Getting through a first semester of med school is a lot. Let that count.

That doesn't mean zero structure. A tiny bit of planning before you leave is exactly what lets you rest without guilt.

## Before you leave: 15 minutes that save you in January

Do these the last day or two before break:

- **Check if anything is actually due.** Skim your school email and syllabus. Some schools have assignments or modules over break, and finding out on January 2 is no fun.
- **Write down where you'll pick up.** One line: the first lecture, the first deck, the first task. Future you will be so grateful!
- **Lower your Anki new cards.** Set new cards to zero, or close to it, in your deck options. New cards turn into reviews for weeks, so this is the single biggest thing that keeps the pile small. (My [Anki beginner guide](../anki-for-med-school-beginners/) explains why.)
- **Pack light.** Leave the textbook pile at home. If you bring it, it just sits in the corner judging you.

## Anki over break: small, or skip it on purpose

This is the question that comes up every year, and there isn't one right answer. Here's what most people seem to land on:

- **A short daily review session** keeps things from piling up. Many students say 20 to 30 minutes in the morning, then done for the day.
- **Some people take a few days fully off,** like the holidays themselves, and accept a bigger review day when they're back. That's okay too!
- **What tends to go badly** is the middle path where you feel guilty about Anki every day but don't do it, and then come back to a mountain.

So pick your plan before you leave, write it on the planner, and then let yourself off the hook. A plan you chose on purpose feels very different from a pile you're avoiding.

## Plan the rest part (yes, really)

It sounds silly to plan rest, but if you don't, break can disappear into scrolling and half-studying. A few things worth putting on paper:

- **Sleep in, on purpose,** at least a few days.
- **The people you want to see.** Family, old friends, the ones who knew you before medicine.
- **Something fun you've been putting off.** A show, a book, a hobby, a trip, an instrument you haven't touched since August.
- **One day with zero medicine at all.** No Anki, no lecture, no "just checking" the next block's schedule.

And if you're heading home to a lot of questions about med school (or one uncle with a mole he'd like you to look at), it's okay to keep your answers short and change the subject!

## Small life admin while you're home

Break is also a great time for the little things that never fit into a block week:

- Doctor, dentist or eye appointments
- Fixing one annoying thing, like a slow laptop or a broken bike
- Restocking basics for January

## The last 2-3 days: ease back in

Instead of going from couch to full study day overnight, give yourself a soft landing:

- **Bump Anki back up slowly,** a few days before classes start.
- **Look at the first week** of your next block's schedule.
- **Set out your first study task** so day one starts easy. If your desk needs a refresh, my [study space setup checklist](../med-student-study-space-setup/) has a quick reset list.
- **Nudge your sleep schedule back** a little each day.

Then, when the new block starts, my [weekly med school study schedule](../med-school-weekly-study-schedule/) can help you lay out that first week.

## How to use the printable

The planner is one page:

- **Before you leave:** what's due, where you'll pick up, Anki new cards, packing light.
- **My rest plan:** sleep, people, something fun, a zero-medicine day.
- **Light upkeep (optional):** your Anki plan, and the days you'll skip completely.
- **Little things that refill me:** walks, calls, a favorite meal, something just for fun.
- **Small life admin:** appointments and fixes.
- **The last 2-3 days:** easing back in.
- **One thing I'm proud of from this semester:** because you should write it down!

## The short version

Make rest the main job of winter break. Before you leave, check what's due, write down where you'll pick up and lower your Anki new cards. Pick a small Anki plan (or planned days off) on purpose, plan real rest, handle a little life admin, and ease back in two or three days early. You made it through a whole semester, and that's worth celebrating!
