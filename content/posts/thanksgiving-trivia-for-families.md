There's a stretch of Thanksgiving afternoon when dinner is done, dessert isn't ready, and half the room is drifting toward their phones. A trivia game fixes that. It's quiet enough for the table, competitive enough for the cousins, and nobody has to get up off the couch.

This printable has 40 questions spread across Thanksgiving history, food, football and a few turkey facts most people get wrong. The answer key sits on its own page, so whoever reads the questions can keep it face down.

## What's in the printable

- **Pages 1-2:** 40 numbered questions with a line for each answer, plus a spot for the team name and score.
- **Page 3:** the answer key, with a couple of short notes so the reader can settle arguments.

Print one question sheet per team and one answer key for the host. Black-and-white printing works fine if your color ink is running low.

## How to play

1. **Split into teams of 2 to 6.** Mix ages on purpose. The kids tend to know the turkey anatomy, and the grown-ups know the presidents.
2. **Pick a reader.** They keep the answer key and read each question out loud, or just hand out the sheets and set a timer.
3. **Set a time limit.** 20 minutes for all 40 works for most groups. If you're playing out loud, give each question about 30 seconds.
4. **Score it.** One point per correct answer. For question 19 (Snoopy's dinner), give half a point for each food, up to one point.
5. **Break ties with a bonus question.** Closest guess wins: "How many pounds of turkey did your family cook today?" The host knows the real number.

## Tips for different groups

### Young kids at the table

Let kids under 8 answer out loud instead of writing, and pair each one with an older reader. Questions 22 to 27 (the turkey facts) and question 39 (Black Friday) are the ones little kids get right most often, so read those out when they need a win.

### A big crowd

With more than 20 people, run it like pub trivia. One reader with a microphone or a loud voice, teams at separate tables, and answer sheets passed to the next team for grading. Swapping sheets keeps everyone honest and takes the pressure off the host.

### Adults only

Add a rule: a team can "double down" on three questions of their choice, earning two points if right and losing one if wrong. It turns a sweet family quiz into something people argue about all through pie.

## Prize ideas that cost nothing

- First pick of the leftovers to take home
- The winning team skips dish duty
- Bragging rights and the last slice of pie
- The winners choose the movie or the next game

## Make it part of the day

If your crowd likes it, keep going. Play a round before dinner while the turkey rests, then save a second round for dessert. Many families keep the same printable every year and track the winning team on the back of the answer key, so the same sheet becomes a little piece of family history.
