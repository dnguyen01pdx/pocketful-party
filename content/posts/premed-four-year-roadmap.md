When you first decide you're premed, the advice comes at you from every direction. Get research! Shadow! Volunteer! Keep a 4.0! Start the MCAT early! It can feel like you're supposed to do all of it, all at once, starting yesterday. If that's where you are right now, please take a breath. Nobody does it all at once, and the people who make it through usually aren't the ones who did the most. They're the ones who kept going steadily.

I'll be honest that my own path wasn't a tidy four-year line. I studied piano performance in college and worked as an ophthalmic technician before I applied, so I definitely didn't follow a perfect schedule! But looking back, I can see what actually mattered and what I'd tell a friend starting out. This roadmap is built from that, plus the advice that keeps getting confirmed in well-liked r/premed threads and advising guides. There's a free two-page checklist at the end.

## Before anything else: this is a sample, not a rule

This plan assumes you'd apply the summer after junior year and start med school right after graduating. Lots of people take one or more gap years instead, and that is completely normal (and often makes for a stronger application). If you're planning a gap year, just slide the junior-year steps later. Prerequisite courses also vary by school, so check the AAMC's Medical School Admission Requirements (MSAR) for the schools you're interested in.

## Freshman year: settle in first

The advice I see repeated most for freshmen is simple: protect your GPA. Applicants say again and again that recovering from a rough first year is one of the most stressful parts of being premed, and it's so much easier to avoid than to fix.

- **Meet your prehealth advisor** early and map out your prerequisite courses together.
- **Figure out how you study best** before you load up your schedule. College classes move differently than high school ones, and that adjustment is real!
- **Join one or two clubs you genuinely like.** Not ten.

By spring or summer, it's a great time to try one clinical role, like volunteering at a hospital or clinic, scribing, or working as a CNA or EMT. And start an activity log right away. AMCAS asks for dates, hours and a contact person for each experience, and it's so much easier to write those down as you go than to dig them up three years later. I really can't stress that one enough!

## Sophomore year: find your groove

Sophomore year is less about trying everything and more about settling into the things you care about.

- **Stay with your clinical role.** Schools tend to value steady involvement over a big burst right before applying.
- **Add some non-clinical service**, ideally something you'd do even if med school weren't the goal. Those are often the experiences that turn into your best essays.
- **Shadow a few physicians**, ideally in different specialties, so you can talk honestly about what doctors actually do.
- **Get to know two or three professors.** Go to office hours, ask questions, let them see how you think. Future you will need letters!

If your grades dip, get help early: tutoring, office hours, a lighter semester. That's not failing; it's just being smart about it.

## Junior year: the application starts taking shape

This is the busiest year, and it's normal for it to feel like a lot.

- **Apply for the AAMC Fee Assistance Program first** if you might qualify. The benefits aren't retroactive, so the order matters.
- **Plan your MCAT date** with enough room for a retake, just in case. For the 2027 testing year, the AAMC lists the registration fee at $360, or $145 with Fee Assistance. Confirm the current fee before you register.
- **Ask for letters** in person or by a thoughtful email, with your CV, a few points you'd love them to mention, and a deadline a few weeks before you really need it.
- **Start drafting.** The AMCAS personal statement gets 5,300 characters, and you can list up to 15 Work and Activities entries. My [personal statement brainstorm post](../personal-statement-brainstorm/) walks through how to find what to write about.

## The summer after junior year: AMCAS

In the 2027 cycle, AMCAS opened May 5, and applicants could submit starting May 28. Submitting early in the window helps, since most schools review applications on a rolling basis. Then secondaries start arriving, and the common advice is to turn each one around in about two weeks. The [month-by-month application timeline](../med-school-application-timeline/) has every 2027 date in one place.

## Senior year: interviews and decisions

Interview season usually runs from the fall into early spring. Practice your answers out loud, keep doing your activities (they still count!), and send updates only where schools say they welcome them. In the spring, decisions come in, and you get to compare cost, financial aid and fit.

## Every single year

A few things stay the same the whole way through:

- **Sleep and grades come first.** Every activity is easier when you aren't running on empty.
- **Log your hours the same week you do them.**
- **Choose things you actually care about.** Readers can tell the difference between a checklist and a genuine interest, and you'll enjoy the next four years a lot more, too!

## The short version

Freshman year, settle in and protect your GPA. Sophomore year, stay steady with clinical work, service and shadowing, and get to know your professors. Junior year, line up Fee Assistance, letters, the MCAT and your drafts. Then submit AMCAS early, take interview season one step at a time, and choose your school in the spring. It's a long road, but you only ever have to take the next step. You've totally got this!
