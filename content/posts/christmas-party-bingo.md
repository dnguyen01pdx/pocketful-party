Bingo is the rare party game that works for a room full of people who don't all know each other. The rules take ten seconds to explain, there's no skill gap between the eight-year-old and the CFO, and someone yelling "BINGO!" across the room is always a good time.

This set has 12 different Christmas bingo cards and a caller's checklist with all 40 words. The cards use words instead of pictures, so they print crisp in black and white and kids who are learning to read can play along.

## What you get

- **12 unique cards.** Every card has a different mix of 24 words around a free "Ho ho ho" center square, so no two players have the same card.
- **A caller's checklist.** All 40 words in alphabetical order. Cut them into slips and draw them from a bowl or a Santa hat, or just tick them off as you call them.

Need more than 12 players? Print the set twice and pair people up on shared cards. Teams of two are more fun anyway, because they argue about whether they missed a word.

## What you'll need

- The printed cards
- Markers: chocolate chips, mini marshmallows, wrapped candies, coins or a pencil
- A bowl or hat for the caller's slips
- A few small prizes (ideas below)

## How to play

1. Hand each player a card and a pile of markers.
2. Everyone marks the free center square.
3. The caller draws one slip at a time and reads it out. Players mark the word if it's on their card.
4. The first player to complete the pattern for that round shouts "Bingo!" and reads their words back so the caller can check them against the drawn slips.
5. Put all the slips back in the bowl and start the next round with a new pattern.

## Five ways to win (so the game lasts)

Playing straight five-in-a-row gets old after two rounds. Rotate through these patterns and save the hardest one for the best prize:

| Round | Pattern | How long it takes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five in a row (across, down or diagonal) | Fast, 8-12 calls |
| 2 | Four corners | Fast, but luck-heavy |
| 3 | Candy cane: the whole left column plus the top row | Medium |
| 4 | Picture frame: every square on the outside edge | Slow, about 25 calls |
| 5 | Blackout: every square on the card | The big finale |

## Prize ideas

Small and silly beats expensive every time:

- A candy cane or a hot cocoa packet for each round winner
- First pick in the gift exchange
- An ugly sweater (bonus points if it's from the thrift store)
- Getting to pick the next Christmas movie
- "Skip one chore" coupons for family parties

For an office party, ask everyone to bring a $5 wrapped item and let bingo winners choose from the pile.

## Tips for big groups

- **Use a microphone or a loud caller.** In a big room, words get lost. Say each one twice.
- **Keep a "called" list visible.** Write the called words on a whiteboard or chalkboard so late arrivals can catch up.
- **Plan for disputes.** The caller's slips decide everything. If a player marked a word that wasn't called, their bingo doesn't count and the round keeps going.
- **Mix in a few quick rounds.** Bingo rounds take five to ten minutes. Three or four rounds is the sweet spot before people want food again.

## Playing with young kids

Pair non-readers with an older buddy, or read each word slowly and point to a picture of it on a phone. Kids also love being the caller, especially if they get to wear the Santa hat the slips are drawn from.
